## Scope and files

Unit 4, Transoceanic Interconnections, covers c. 1450–1750 within the AP World collection's overall c. 1200–1750 scope. Its eight official topic sheets follow the College Board framework order:

| 4.3 Columbian Exchange | `4.3-columbian-exchange.txt` |
| 4.4 Empires Established | `4.4-empires-established.txt` |
| 4.5 Empires Maintained | `4.5-empires-maintained.txt` |
| 4.6 State Challenges | `4.6-state-challenges.txt` |
| 4.7 Social Hierarchies | `4.7-social-hierarchies.txt` |
| 4.8 Continuity and Change | `4.8-continuity-change.txt` |

Each topic is one two-page, 48-line sheet. Unit 4 therefore contains eight sheets, 16 pages, and 384 note lines. Sources are stored under `website/public/notes/unit-4/` and `website/public/notes/unit-4/Colored/`; marked copies preserve the original decoded note text. The full AP World set comprises 26 official topics plus two Unit 3 supplements: 28 sheets, 56 pages, and 1,344 lines.

## Content and repair record

The notes are concise study summaries, not exhaustive treatments, official College Board materials, or guarantees of exam performance. The official framework determines required scope; examples in it and in course resources are not exhaustive checklists. The Unit 4 set was reviewed against the identified targeted-repair findings. Repairs address the specified omissions and weaknesses in topics 4.3–4.7 without adding a supplement or changing the 48-line sheet format. Topics 4.1, 4.2, and 4.8 were not changed in that repair pass, nor was the labor/slavery page of 4.4. Refer to the specific source notes and limitations below rather than extending an individual claim beyond its evidence.

| Topic | Coverage and evidence retained |

The released [2023 Set 2 scoring guidelines](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap23-sg-world-history-modern-set-2.pdf), SAQ 3 (PDF pp. 5–6) and LEQ 2 (pp. 19–23), were used to assess factual sufficiency for technology/religion/syncretism and cross-cultural exploration/trade. The notes supply relevant evidence and explanatory links; that is not a scored student response or a forecast of future exams. No secure AP Classroom questions were used.

## Source ledger

1. **College Board, AP World History: Modern Course and Exam Description**, effective Fall 2026, Unit 4 PDF pp. 85–98 / printed pp. 78–91. Framework scope and required developments; not treated as an exhaustive case list. [PDF](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-world-history-modern-course-and-exam-description.pdf)
2. **UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Forts and Castles of Ghana.** Fortified coastal posts and Elmina's 1482 construction. The notes use Elmina as a specific Portuguese coastal post, not UNESCO's broader first-contact framing. [Record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/34/)
3. **The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Trade Relations among European and African Nations.** African trade networks, rulers, fortified posts, and mercantile relationships. [Essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/trade-relations-among-european-and-african-nations)
4. **OER Project, Oceanic Empires, 1450 to 1750.** Ports, forts, naval-commercial coercion, and chartered-company financing; read alongside other research for the limited cartaz enforcement point. [Article](https://www.oerproject.com/oer-materials/oer-media/html-articles/ap-world-history/unit4/oceanic-empires-1450-to-1750)
5. **The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Christianity and Kongo Visual Culture.** Kongo Christianity, royal legitimacy, local reinterpretation, conflict, and enslavement. [Essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/christianity-kongo-visual-culture)
6. **Columbia Asia for Educators, 1635 Tokugawa edict.** Translated primary source concerning missionary investigations, punishments, and restrictions on some direct foreign purchasing. [PDF](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/ps/japan/tokugawa_edicts_foreigners.pdf)
7. **The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Kongo Crucifix, accession 1999.295.7.** Sixteenth–seventeenth-century object and curatorial interpretation; the notes cautiously say some crucifixes related to ancestral beliefs, not that this applied universally. [Object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/318323)
8. **Jason W. Warren, Encyclopaedia Britannica, King Philip's War.** Metacom's Wampanoag identity, land grievances, 1675–1676 conflict, Native allies, displacement, and enslavement. The concise notes do not imply all Native peoples took one side or disappeared after the conflict. [Article](https://www.britannica.com/event/King-Philips-War)
9. **Robert Lewis, Encyclopaedia Britannica, Roxelana.** The Haseki complex, begun by 1539, included schools and a hospital. This supports a specific example of Hurrem's public-works patronage, not a generalization about all Ottoman women. [Article](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Roxelana)
10. **Journal of Indian History (2022), “The Portuguse Cartaz System in the Maritime Trade at Canannore.”** Supports the limited point that the pass exposed noncompliant vessels to search or seizure. Its sweeping claims of Portuguese dominion and erroneous company attribution are not adopted. [PDF](https://jihku.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/9.pdf)
11. **The Met, Europe and the Age of Exploration**, and [Fiveable Unit 4](https://fiveable.me/ap-world/unit-4) and its relevant topic readings: navigation/exploration breadth, with the framework controlling required scope. [Met essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/europe-and-the-age-of-exploration)
12. **OER Project, The Columbian Exchange.** Substantive reading on directional transfers, epidemics and labor. Its simplified immunity account and broad use of “enslavement” for encomienda are not substituted for the notes' more precise qualifications. No whole-trade embarked/arrival estimate is copied into the 1450–1750 sheets. [Article](https://www.oerproject.com/oer-materials/oer-media/html-articles/ap-world-history/unit4/the-columbian-exchange)
13. **Library of Congress, Europe Claims America.** Colonial coercion, adapted Andean labor obligations and plantation contexts. [Exhibit](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/1492/eurocla.html)
14. **Columbia Asia for Educators**, [silver and trade](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/chinawh/web/s5/s5_4b.html), [related teaching activity](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/neh/course7/activity2.html), and [Qing emperors](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/qing/emperors.html): exchange mechanisms, Chinese demand and multiethnic imperial legitimacy, with later reign events kept outside the topic's cutoff.
15. **The Met**, [Art of the Asante Kingdom](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/art-of-the-asante-kingdom) and [Ana Nzinga](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/ana-nzinga-queen-of-ndongo): African commercial/political agency and diplomacy/warfare, not uniform African experiences or uninterrupted resistance to all trade.
16. **National Park Service, The Road to Revolt**, and **SciELO, Palmares study**: Pueblo religious/labor grievances and organized resistance to enslavement. [Pueblo PDF](https://www.nps.gov/elca/learn/historyculture/upload/The-Road-To-Revolt-508.pdf) · [Palmares PDF](https://socialsciences.scielo.org/pdf/s_nec/v2nse/scs_a04.pdf)
17. **Smarthistory, casta paintings teaching guide.** Colonial representations of ancestry/status, not proof of one rigid universal lived social ladder. [Guide](https://smarthistory.org/teaching-guide-sixteen-casta-paintings/)

Freemanpedia's substantive Unit 4 content was not accessible at sufficient depth during the research pass. No claim depends on unread images, video or its extracted overview shell.

## Palette and repository verification

`website/scripts/build.mjs` is the ordered topic manifest and defines the explicit `unit4Palette` selection. Unit 4 uses the eleven highlight inks from FBLA: dark/lime green, purple, orange, yellow, pink, light blue, hot pink, red, dark blue and medium blue, plus neutral pencil. The `.unit-4-palette` scope gives `(y)` yellow `#ebc600`, `(mb)` medium blue `#287db3`, `(db)` dark blue `#173f88`, and `(r)` red `#df3034`. Legacy Units 1–3, AP Lang and Algebra keep `(y)` medium blue; their source files are not recolored. Brown belongs to the legacy palette, not Unit 4.

All ten section inks occur across Unit 4 with varied neighboring heading palettes; light blue identifies external topic/page titles. Every marked file has exactly the original 48 decoded lines, with neutral punctuation and at most two highlighted supporting words. The importer validates and renders the approved sources rather than re-authoring them. `tests/unit4-checks.mjs` protects the topic list, source structure, repaired facts and palette membership; browser checks cover HTTP/file downloads, mobile/RTL, screen/print color scoping and physical page counts. Structural tests cannot independently certify historical accuracy.

The initial repository integration passed `npm run validate` and all **71 Playwright tests**, including an exported offline preview, screen/print color scoping, no-JavaScript downloads, and 56-page full/16-page Unit 4/two-page topic PDF counts. The local browser run used an existing Chromium executable via an external configuration override; no machine-specific launch path is checked in. Existing Unit 1–3 note sources and other subjects' generated pages were preserved. This is a record of the integration checks, not a promise that future edits remain validated without rerunning them.

## Limits and intended use

The targeted review replaced a vague timar sentence with the better-supported French Fronde example; this does not imply timars disappeared or that French and Ottoman institutions were equivalent. Hurrem's example is specific patronage, not a claim that she personally taught or treated patients. Metacom's outcome refers to harms to defeated Native communities, not the end of Indigenous peoples. The sources support selected factual claims and a concise review set; they do not make these notes exhaustive, replace primary-source analysis or practice, or guarantee an AP score.

The existing Unit 3 audit is a historical record of its earlier version and should not be rewritten as if it audited Unit 4. No private local file paths are needed to use this guide; cited URLs are portable public references.
