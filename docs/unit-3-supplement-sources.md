# Unit 3 supplements: source and coverage notes

The supplemental material consists of two 48-line sheets: **Military and Imperial Government** and **Beliefs, Legitimacy, and Comparison**. Together they contain four 24-line pages and 96 ASCII note lines, with a 65-character maximum. The four main topic sheets are unchanged.

**Color coding is applied without changing the draft text.** Both supplements use short one- or two-word highlights tied mainly to their section's Overview color, with matching full header colors, neutral prefixes/punctuation, and a few consistent cross-references. The palette contains the existing eight inks plus orange `(or)` (`#e67e22`). From 3.4 page 2's Revenue and local elites onward, orange replaces brown; earlier pages retain their original brown. The two supplemental drafts' plain originals remain unchanged by this color pass.

## Scope and selection

The [College Board AP World History: Modern framework](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-world-history-modern-course-and-exam-description.pdf), printed Unit 3 pages 69–72 / PDF pages 76–79, supplies the learning objectives. [Fiveable's Unit 3 course](https://fiveable.me/ap-world/unit-3), [OER Project's AP Unit 3](https://www.oerproject.com/World-History-AP/Unit-3), and [Freemanpedia 3.2](https://www.freeman-pedia.com/32empiresadministration) were used to find meaningful omissions beyond the framework's short illustrative list. Historical references check the mechanisms and dates rather than treating any one review page as an exhaustive or infallible checklist.

The four supplemental pages are organized as follows:

| Sheet/page | Focus | Gaps and foundations addressed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplement 1, page 1 | Military centralization and expansion | Abbas's ghulams/Qizilbash counterweight, royal revenue and Isfahan, Ottoman North Africa/Egypt, caliphal claims and holy cities, Morocco–Songhai rivalry, gunpowder and logistical limits. |
| Supplement 1, page 2 | Revenue, service elites, and communal rule | Tax farming, jagirs, mit'a, Ming silver taxes, French intendants, Russian noble–serf relations, salaried samurai, millet variation, nonexclusive communal jurisdiction, jizya liability and protected status. |
| Supplement 2, page 1 | Belief, authority, and religious legitimacy | Indulgences and justification, Calvinist predestination, English royal supremacy, Trent, Sunni/Twelver authority, divine right and Heaven's mandate, Inca sacred ancestry, Songhai Islam, Qing Buddhist patronage, Russian Orthodoxy. |
| Supplement 2, page 2 | Accommodation, settlement, and comparative authority | Rajput cooperation, the Hindu-majority context, Akbarnama and patronage, Tokugawa attendance/hostages, Augsburg and Westphalia, the growth of Christianity, Mexica ritual/tribute, and American labor/goods/gunpowder comparisons. |

These are selected evidence and explanatory mechanisms, not a prediction of future questions or a claim that every optional case from every textbook must be memorized. Earlier events are included only where needed to explain early modern institutions or beliefs. American cases are bounded to pre-European-conquest imperial rule, not extended through 1750 as independent empires.

## Safavid forces, revenue, and chronology

- [Fiveable: Shah Abbas I](https://fiveable.me/ap-world/key-terms/shah-abbas-i) connects ghulams, Qizilbash power, crown revenue, the silk trade, and Isfahan to imperial consolidation.
- [Encyclopaedia Iranica: Abbas I](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/abbas-i/) supports Abbas's 1588–1629 reign, Caucasian recruits, personal royal service, the counterweight to tribal chiefs, royal revenue, and building in Isfahan. Recruitment included enslaved converts as well as some voluntary recruits; the word **included** is deliberate.
- [Iranica: Safavid army](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/army-iii/) distinguishes royal ghulam forces, continuing tribal forces, and other arms. Ghulams are not identified as simply a musket-infantry replacement for all cavalry.
- [Iranica: Chaldiran](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/calderan-battle/) places the 1514 battle under Ismail I, not Abbas I, and describes supply problems that forced the Ottomans out of Tabriz after victory. The chronological distinction is explicit in the notes.

## Ottoman/African reach and religious authority

- [Metropolitan Museum: The Art of the Ottomans before 1600](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-art-of-the-ottomans-before-1600) supports the conquest of Mamluk Egypt in 1517, the Holy Cities, and the expansion of Ottoman authority and culture across regions. Morocco is not presented as an Ottoman province.
- [OER Project: Legitimizing and Consolidating Power](https://www.oerproject.com/OER-Materials/OER-Media/HTML-Articles/AP-World-History/Unit3/Legitimizing-and-Consolidating-Power) supplies comparative religious claims and explicitly notes that not all Muslims accepted Ottoman claims. The notes do not assert a formal, universally accepted transfer of the caliphate in 1517.
- [Britannica: Songhai empire](https://www.britannica.com/place/Songhai-empire) supports succession conflicts, Moroccan pressure on trade/salt resources, and firearm-assisted defeat at Tondibi in 1591. Firearms are a contributing mechanism, not the only cause. The separate Tabriz example supplies the logistical limit rather than inaccurately asserting that Moroccan forces never controlled Songhai's cities.

## Revenue and elite dependence

- [Hansraj College, University of Delhi: The Mughal Jagirdari and Mansabdari System](https://www.hansrajcollege.ac.in/hCPanel/uploads/elearning/elearning_document/Mughal_Jagirdari_and_Mansabdari_System.pdf) distinguishes revenue assignments from ownership and explains how reassignment checked local roots. Hereditary/nontransferable watan jagirs are exceptions, so the note says **many**, not all, were reassigned.
- [Britannica: Intendant](https://www.britannica.com/topic/intendant-French-official) describes provincial royal agents and their role in reducing reliance on or displacing other local authorities. The notes do not claim the French monarchy abolished noble influence.
- [OER Project: From Muscovy to the Russian Empire](https://www.oerproject.com/OER-Materials/OER-Media/HTML-Articles/AP-World-History/Unit3/From-Muscovy-to-the-Russian-Empire) supplies the service/privilege relationship and Orthodox support. The notes do not claim the Romanovs first invented serfdom, that every peasant was a serf, or that Russia's whole economy rested on Siberian fur tribute.
- Ming silver collection and salaried samurai are named CED illustrations. The silver line explains why an agricultural household needed access to markets; the samurai line distinguishes paid service from the separate attendance mechanism.

## Millet arrangements and jizya

- Efrat Aviv, [Oxford Bibliographies: Millet System](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62361/reference-article/554566607), describes limited communal affairs under Ottoman supremacy.
- [Cambridge: Millet in Ottoman Documents](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/nationalities-papers/article/millet-in-ottoman-documents-religious-confessional-or-ethnicnational-collectives-of-nonmuslims/0D85ED642595B3726C1883E37A613220) cautions against projecting a uniform nineteenth-century corporate system backward. The notes explicitly retain local/temporal variation.
- [UCLA MANAS: Aurangzeb's Fatwa on Jizya](https://southasia.ucla.edu/history-politics/mughals-and-medieval/aurangzeb/aurangzebs-fatwa-jizya/), translated by Anver Emon, provides a period legal text on eligibility, means-based rates, and exemptions. It is a Mughal legal source, not evidence of identical implementation in every Ottoman locality.
- Communal authority and jizya are kept distinct: the tax itself did not grant self-government, and protected worship did not imply equal legal status. Community courts are not presented as the exclusive jurisdiction for all non-Muslim cases.

## Religious definitions and political outcomes

- The [Catholic Catechism, 1471–1473](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_two/section_two/chapter_two/article_4/x_indulgences.html), distinguishes temporal penalties from guilt already forgiven. This modern official definition clarifies the doctrinal distinction; it does not show that all sixteenth-century sales practices were identical or that Catholic doctrine sold salvation.
- Britannica's [predestination](https://www.britannica.com/topic/predestination), [Sunni](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Sunni), [Shi'a](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Shii), and [Twelver Shi'a](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Twelver-Shia) articles support the definitions. The early succession dispute is context for the later Ottoman–Safavid rivalry, not a new split beginning in 1450–1750. Safavid shahs are not described as the twelve Imams.
- [Fiveable: Peace of Augsburg](https://fiveable.me/ap-euro/key-terms/peace-of-augsburg) explains the 1555 Catholic/Lutheran territorial settlement and the later recognition of Calvinism at Westphalia. This is used as evidence of religion/state interaction, not as an expansion of the AP World requirement into the entire AP European syllabus.
- [Yale Avalon: Treaty of Westphalia](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/17th_century/westphal.asp) provides treaty text on imperial estates and confessional rights. The notes avoid claiming that the settlements immediately created modern sovereign nation-states or equal religious freedom for everyone.

## Art, accommodation, and audience

- [Columbia Asia for Educators: Qing emperors](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/qing/emperors.html) explains different representations to Han, Mongol, and Tibetan constituencies. Buddhist patronage is not restricted to a Han-Confucian legitimacy model; later Qing conquest dates are not moved into the pre-1750 expansion story.
- [V&A: Akbarnama collection record](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O9441/akbar-painting-kesav-kalan/) identifies Akbar as commissioner and Abu'l Fazl as historian. Royal funding is a reason to consider a source's purpose, not proof that every court account is false.
- [2024 AP World Set 1 scoring guidelines](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-sg-world-history-modern-set-1.pdf), SAQ 1(c), accepts explanations connecting Mughal interaction to loyalty and subjects' contributions. [Set 2](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-sg-world-history-modern-set-2.pdf), SAQ 1(c), accepts historical writing/art as legitimation. These are public examples of assessable reasoning, not frequency predictions.
- Mexica human sacrifice, tribute lists, Inca sun temples, and rotating labor are used as state mechanisms with explicit sacred or resource links. These were already introduced in Unit 1; their Unit 3 use is comparison/application, not an assertion that imperial institutions arose only after 1450 or survived independently through 1750.
