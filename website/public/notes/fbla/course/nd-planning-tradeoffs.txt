o Peer-to-peer or client-server
-> Peer-to-peer (P2P) lets endpoints share resources directly.
-> P2P avoids dedicated server cost for small, simple groups.
-> Each peer may manage users, rights, and copies separately.
-> Shared resource availability depends on its host being online.
-> Decentralized rights and backups hinder oversight and restore.
-> Compromised peer or account can expose shared resources.
-> Client-server centralizes shared services and administration.
-> Central policy is consistent but adds server dependencies.
-> Size capacity, backup, and failover for critical server roles.
o Cloud service benefits and costs
-> Cloud services provide computing or management over a network.
-> Elastic capacity meets demand without local hardware buying.
-> IaaS leaves OS to customer; PaaS and SaaS shift more layers.
-> Customer owns data and identities; contract terms govern.
-> Usage, storage, support, and transfer create recurring costs.
-> Connectivity failure interrupts dependent workflows.
-> Review identity, access, data protection, recovery, export.
-> Local systems need staff and facilities but may work offline.
-> Compare lifecycle costs and ownership, not hardware price.
-> Portability depends on formats, contracts, and dependencies.

o Cost, throughput, and availability
-> Constraints include budget, space, and compatibility.
-> Business drivers include capacity, growth, and recovery.
-> Measure busy-hour throughput and identify bottlenecks.
-> Bandwidth costs money and may not fix server or app delay.
-> Independent redundant links and devices improve availability.
-> Extra paths need routes, tests, monitoring, and skilled staff.
-> Star's central device can fail without redundancy.
-> Mesh increases path choices and cabling/management complexity.
-> Topology labels alone do not guarantee performance or uptime.
o Service targets and design choice
-> A service level agreement (SLA) states measurable promises.
-> Define service, measurement window, exclusions, and reporting.
-> Provider uptime is not end-to-end application uptime.
-> Map power, DNS, identity, carrier, and storage dependencies.
-> Health probes can remove unhealthy backends from flows.
-> A probe may miss an untested dependency failure.
-> Session persistence keeps client affinity but can skew load.
-> Model options against cost, throughput, recovery, and staff.
-> Record assumptions, options, owners, and review triggers.
-> Review targets when business impact or dependencies change.
