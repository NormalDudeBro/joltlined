o Build a reliable relationship
-> Relevant, dependable service can build customer trust.
-> Agree on follow-up purpose, channel, and timing with consent.
-> Record only needed contact details and protect them.
-> Confirm owner, date, and promised deliverable.
-> Keep promises or notify buyer and reset expectations.
-> Ask whether the outcome met the stated need.
-> Resolve issues or route them to someone who can help.
-> Honor opt-out and communication preferences promptly.
-> Do not guarantee loyalty or outcomes beyond your control.
o Organize buyer-centered content
-> A coherent sequence helps listeners follow the decision.
-> Useful order: greet, discover, confirm, recommend, respond.
-> Then suggest fit, decide next step, and follow up.
-> Transitions explain why the next point matters.
-> Example: connect a stated delay to the relevant workflow.
-> Keep sections tied to confirmed buyer priorities.
-> Avoid feature dumps and repeated claims.
-> Clear organization aids recovery after a question.
-> Separate verified facts, examples, and recommendations.
-> Adapt order when buyer information changes priorities.

o Manage time and transitions
-> FBLA gives 3 minutes for setup, then 7 for presentation.
-> Time segments are exclusive; setup time cannot extend pitch.
-> There is no separate question-and-answer segment.
-> Judges may ask during presentation; reserve room to respond.
-> Answer, then use a short transition back to the thread.
-> Practice aloud and rehearse recovering from a missed point.
-> Clear organization helps restore the thread after a mistake.
-> A brief outline supports flow without a rigid script.
-> Check time by rehearsal; do not rush a buyer's question.
o Coordinate team handoffs
-> All team members must actively participate in delivery.
-> Assign section ownership and agree on handoff cues.
-> A handoff links the prior point to the next speaker's role.
-> Share verified facts so offer descriptions stay consistent.
-> Face the audience and allow each speaker to be heard.
-> Practice missed-cue recovery without blaming a teammate.
-> Do not speak over a teammate answering a judge.
-> A teammate may clarify; keep one coherent answer.
-> Rehearse transitions under the actual seven-minute limit.
-> Registered competitors prepare their own real event entry.
