o Wireless Generations and Bands
-> 802.11b uses 2.4 GHz DSSS; max PHY rate is 11 Mb/s.
-> 802.11a uses 5 GHz OFDM; max PHY rate is 54 Mb/s.
-> 802.11g uses 2.4 GHz OFDM; max PHY rate is 54 Mb/s.
-> 802.11n (Wi-Fi 4) supports 2.4/5 GHz and MIMO.
-> 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5) operates in 5 GHz bands.
-> 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) supports 2.4 and 5 GHz operation.
-> Wi-Fi 6E adds 6 GHz where permitted and supported.
-> 802.11be (Wi-Fi 7) adds multi-link in capable devices.
-> Max PHY rates require matching channel and radio capability.
o Wireless Range and Throughput
-> There is no single universal indoor Wi-Fi range figure.
-> Band, walls, antennas, power, and receivers affect reach.
-> 2.4 GHz often travels farther through walls than 5/6 GHz.
-> 2.4 GHz is often more congested; local spectrum matters.
-> Channel width, streams, and interference affect PHY rate.
-> App throughput is below PHY rate due to overhead/contention.
-> Clients share airtime; slow stations reduce capacity.
-> Regulatory domain limits channels and transmit power.
-> Survey user locations and load before estimating coverage.
-> A repeater can extend reach but consumes airtime or adds hops.

o Ethernet PHY and Medium
-> 10BASE-T is 10 Mb/s over twisted-pair copper, up to 100 m.
-> 100BASE-TX is 100 Mb/s copper, up to 100 m channel.
-> 1000BASE-T carries 1 Gb/s over four pairs, up to 100 m.
-> 10GBASE-T can reach 100 m on Cat 6A compliant channel.
-> Cat 6 10GBASE-T reach depends on channel and alien crosstalk.
-> 1000BASE-SX uses multimode fiber; reach varies by grade.
-> 1000BASE-LX reach depends on optics and fiber type.
-> 10GBASE-SR uses multimode fiber; distance depends on fiber.
-> IEEE PHY, optics, connectors, and loss set fiber reach.
o Routing and Wireless Protection
-> OSPF is link-state routing within an autonomous system.
-> OSPF routers build topology databases and calculate paths.
-> BGP exchanges inter-AS prefixes and paths for policy.
-> BGP is an IETF Standards Track protocol.
-> EIGRP uses DUAL, a distance-vector-based routing algorithm.
-> RFC 7868 is Informational, not an IETF Standards Track spec.
-> WPA2 deployments can use AES-CCMP; avoid legacy TKIP.
-> WPA3-Personal SAE strengthens resistance to offline guessing.
-> WPA3 connections require Protected Management Frames.
-> Negotiated security depends on AP/client configuration.
