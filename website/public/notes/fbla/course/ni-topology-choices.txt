o LAN, MAN and WAN scope
-> LAN means local area network across a limited site or campus.
-> A MAN spans a metropolitan or city region.
-> A WAN joins distant sites across larger geographic areas.
-> WAN links often depend on telecom or Internet providers.
-> Routers connect local networks across inter-site links.
-> Geographic scope does not specify ownership or a fixed speed.
-> WAN distance often increases latency and service cost.
-> Provider outages can affect several connected locations.
-> A VPN protects a path; it cannot prevent provider outages.
o WAN implications and scale
-> A company WAN may link branches, headquarters and datacenters.
-> Applications sensitive to delay need realistic WAN testing.
-> Bandwidth and latency are distinct performance properties.
-> A MAN may be provider-operated or organization-managed.
-> LAN typically supports local switching and local services.
-> WAN agreements specify capacity and availability terms.
-> Backup circuits help only if routes and infrastructure differ.
-> A remote site still relies on its local LAN and gateway.
-> Choose scope from users, geography and application needs.
-> No topology name guarantees a particular reliability level.

o Choose topology for requirements
-> A small office often uses a switched star for easy management.
-> A critical site may add diverse uplinks for availability.
-> A partial mesh trades extra paths against cost and complexity.
-> Legacy bus is rarely a new Ethernet installation choice.
-> Compare failure impact, growth, traffic and operator skills.
-> A central star switch is a possible single point of failure.
-> Separate power and physical routes improve path independence.
-> No topology is best for every business requirement.
-> Physical cabling choices also constrain speed and distance.
o Wireless network size options
-> A small LAN may use an integrated Wi-Fi router or one AP.
-> An access point bridges wireless clients to a wired LAN.
-> Large campuses deploy multiple managed access points.
-> AP placement should account for coverage and client density.
-> Wired backhaul avoids radio retransmission at every hop.
-> Managed systems coordinate settings and may support roaming.
-> Cloud management adds provider and Internet dependencies.
-> Survey results vary with walls, interference and location.
-> More APs do not automatically create better coverage.
-> All network sizes need secure settings and monitoring.
