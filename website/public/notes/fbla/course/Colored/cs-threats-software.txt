(pen)o (li)Threat Actors and Motives(pen)
(pen)-> Insiders have organizational access; outsiders do not.
(pen)-> Either may act deliberately or cause harm through error.
(pen)-> Financial gain can motivate theft, extortion, or fraud.
(pen)-> Espionage seeks protected information for strategic advantage.
(pen)-> Data theft may support resale, fraud, or later intrusion.
(pen)-> Disruption or ideology may motivate service or data attacks.
(pen)-> Assess capability, access, opportunity, and target as well.
(pen)-> Motive is a hypothesis, not proof of actor identity.
(pen)-> Controls should address behavior and access, not stereotypes.
(pen)o (p)Virus Varieties and Other Malware(pen)
(pen)-> (p)Malware(pen) is software used to harm, spy, extort, or control.
(pen)-> A (p)virus(pen) attaches to a host file or boot area to spread.
(pen)-> File (p)virus(pen) infects executable files when they run.
(pen)-> Boot-sector (p)virus(pen) targets startup records or boot media.
(pen)-> Polymorphic viruses vary code/signature during replication.
(pen)-> Variation may evade simple signatures; behavior checks help.
(pen)-> Trojan masquerades as benign software to induce execution.
(pen)-> A worm self-propagates across systems or networks.
(pen)-> Logic bomb activates when a time/event condition is met.
(pen)-> One (p)malware(pen) sample may combine more than one behavior.
