o Open with relevance
-> A greeting starts a buyer conversation respectfully.
-> Use context, introduce yourself, and ask permission.
-> A relevant opener leads naturally to a discovery question.
-> Example: ask what a new-hire welcome must accomplish.
-> The example is a prompt, not a memorized contest script.
-> Match formality to setting; do not presume familiarity.
-> A greeting is not a feature list or forced joke.
-> Then invite the customer's perspective and listen.
-> FBLA top band rewards an original smooth transition.
o Find the need
-> Need: outcome, task, or constraint the buyer must address.
-> Want: preferred attribute; it may matter, but verify why.
-> Ask open questions about goals and current process.
-> Probe frequency, friction, impact, and desired outcome.
-> Follow up: what happens when the step is delayed?
-> Closed questions confirm facts; they rarely reveal context.
-> Listen fully; paraphrase and invite correction.
-> Separate what was said from your interpretation.
-> Do not manufacture needs or lead the buyer to agree.
-> Follow-up questions test context, not just product interest.
o Overview
-> Map people and priorities
-> Confirm before advising
o Map people and priorities
-> User, buyer, payer, approver, and reviewer can differ.
-> Ask who uses, funds, approves, or checks compatibility.
-> One stakeholder's preference may not represent the group.
-> Discover budget, timing, workflow, and decision process.
-> Ask permission before recording contact preferences.
-> Example: manager funds; staff use; IT checks access.
-> Confirm each role's relevant outcome and constraint.
-> Do not collect unrelated personal information.
-> Stakeholder map helps test fit, not force consensus.
o Confirm before advising
-> Summarize the priority and ask if you understood correctly.
-> Resolve conflicting priorities before recommending.
-> Connect a recommendation only to a confirmed need.
-> A colorful dashboard is a want until purpose is clear.
-> It may become relevant if it improves needed readability.
-> Stop discovery when enough is known to assess fit.
-> If the offer mismatches, qualify or recommend no sale.
-> Ask permission to move from discovery to recommendation.
-> An accurate summary reduces mistaken assumptions.
-> Confirm buyer context again if new facts change the fit.
