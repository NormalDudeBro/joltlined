o Millet and jizya distinctions
-> Ottoman millet arrangements recognized religious communities.
-> Leaders could oversee marriage, schools, and charity.
-> Community authority remained subject to Ottoman rule.
-> Millet practices varied; they were not one fixed system.
-> Jizya was a poll tax on eligible adult non-Muslim men.
-> Women and children were exempt; rates could vary by means.
-> Dhimmi protection did not mean equal legal status.
-> Jizya did not itself grant communal self-government.
o Revenue and service systems
-> Ming silver taxes tied peasants to commercial markets.
-> Tokugawa samurai often became salaried officials.
-> Inca mit'a required labor; Ottoman tax farming raised cash.
-> Inca quipu recorded labor and goods for state administration.
-> Mughal jagirs granted revenue rights, not ownership of land.
o Imperial religion and art
-> Inca rulers claimed descent from Inti, the sun god.
-> Cuzco's sun temple made Inca sacred kingship visible.
-> Mexica sacrifice linked rulers to maintaining cosmic order.
-> Askia Muhammad promoted Islam to legitimize Songhai rule.

o Conquest and its limits
-> Morocco defeated Songhai at Tondibi in 1591.
-> Moroccan firearms helped defeat Songhai forces.
-> Distance weakened Moroccan control of the Niger valley.
-> Aztec and Inca empires expanded without gunpowder.
-> Weapons aided conquest; logistics helped sustain control.
o Cultural patronage and authority
-> Mughal Akbar recruited Rajput allies to reduce resistance.
-> Hindu elites supplied Mughal revenue and military service.
-> Mughal and Rajput painters exchanged artistic techniques.
-> Akbar commissioned the Akbarnama to glorify his reign.
-> Court histories praised dynasties; purpose shaped portrayal.
-> Accommodation could secure loyalty without legal equality.
o Belief and comparative rule
-> Protestant and Catholic reforms both spread Christianity.
-> Qing rulers used Confucian rites and Tibetan Buddhism.
-> Multiple traditions helped Qing rulers court diverse elites.
-> In theory, Heaven's mandate depended on moral rule.
-> Tokugawa alternate attendance tied daimyo to Edo.
-> Like Versailles, it made regional elites easier to supervise.
