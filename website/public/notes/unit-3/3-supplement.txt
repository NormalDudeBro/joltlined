o Millet and communal authority
-> Ottoman millets denoted religious, not territorial, groups.
-> Some religious leaders managed local communal affairs.
-> Christian and Jewish courts could handle marriage matters.
-> Communities supported worship, schools, and charity.
-> Community authority remained subject to Ottoman rule.
-> These were evolving practices, not a uniform fixed system.
o Jizya and protected status
-> Jizya was a poll tax on eligible adult non-Muslim men.
-> Women and children were generally exempt.
-> Poverty or disability could also bring exemptions.
-> Rates could differ with wealth and local tax practices.
-> Dhimmi status offered protection with legal inequality.
-> Jizya did not itself grant communal self-government.
o Accommodation and its limits
-> Ottoman communities could also use Islamic courts.
-> Community autonomy was limited, not religious equality.
-> Non-Muslims faced taxes and legal restrictions.
-> Mughal Akbar abolished jizya in 1564.
-> Aurangzeb restored Mughal jizya in 1679; policy varied.

o Gunpowder and interstate rivalry
-> Morocco defeated Songhai at Tondibi in 1591.
-> Moroccan firearms overcame Songhai's larger forces.
-> Distance weakened Moroccan control of the Niger valley.
-> Shah Abbas I expanded Safavid musketeers and artillery.
-> Abbas checked Qizilbash tribal warriors with new troops.
o Revenue and military service
-> Ottoman timars assigned tax revenue for cavalry service.
-> Mughal jagirs granted revenue rights, not ownership of land.
-> Mansab ranks set Mughal officers' status and troop duties.
-> Ming silver taxes tied peasants to commercial markets.
-> Silver scarcity could raise the real burden of Ming taxes.
-> Ottoman inflation strained cash pay and military finances.
o Governing imperial elites
-> Tokugawa alternate attendance required daimyo stays in Edo.
-> Daimyo families stayed in Edo as political hostages.
-> Tokugawa samurai often became salaried officials.
-> Qing emperors used Confucian rites and Tibetan Buddhism.
-> Multiple traditions helped Qing rulers court diverse elites.
-> In theory, Heaven's mandate depended on moral rule.
