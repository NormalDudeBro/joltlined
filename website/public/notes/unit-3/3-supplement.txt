o Millet and jizya mechanisms
-> Millets let non-Muslim leaders manage worship and family law.
-> Delegation reduced the need for direct Ottoman administration.
-> Community authority remained subject to Ottoman rule.
-> Jizya was a poll tax on eligible adult non-Muslim men.
-> Dhimmi protection allowed worship, not legal equality.
-> Jizya did not itself grant communal self-government.
o Revenue and elite dependence
-> Tax farmers advanced cash for rights to collect state taxes.
-> Ottoman tax farming raised cash but empowered collectors.
-> Mughal jagirs assigned tax income, not ownership of land.
-> Many jagirs were transferable, keeping nobles dependent.
-> Inca mit'a required rotating labor rather than cash payments.
-> Ming silver taxes pushed peasants to sell crops for money.
o Sacred authority and legitimacy
-> Divine right held that monarchs received authority from God.
-> This cast obedience to a king as a religious duty.
-> Qing rulers claimed Heaven's mandate, conditional on virtue.
-> Inca sun temples displayed rulers' claimed divine ancestry.
-> Askia Muhammad promoted Islam to legitimize Songhai rule.

o Reformation and church authority
-> Indulgences remitted punishment for already-forgiven sins.
-> Selling indulgences fueled charges of church corruption.
-> Luther taught justification: acceptance by God through faith.
-> The Council of Trent defended doctrine and reformed clergy.
-> Some princes backed Luther to control churches and revenues.
-> Both Protestant and Catholic reforms expanded Christianity.
o Sunni and Shi'a leadership
-> Sunnis recognized the first four caliphs as rightly guided.
-> Shi'a saw Ali and his line as Muhammad's rightful successors.
-> Twelver Shi'a followed twelve Imams as religious authorities.
-> Ottomans backed Sunni clergy; Safavids backed Shi'a clergy.
o Accommodation and central control
-> Mughal Akbar recruited Rajputs, Hindu warrior elites.
-> Their troops and support eased rule over a Hindu majority.
-> Akbar commissioned the Akbarnama as an official history.
-> Funding encouraged authors to portray rulers favorably.
-> Daimyo, Tokugawa lords, paid stipends to samurai warriors.
-> Alternate attendance rotated daimyo stays at Edo's court.
-> Like Versailles, court stays made elites easier to supervise.
